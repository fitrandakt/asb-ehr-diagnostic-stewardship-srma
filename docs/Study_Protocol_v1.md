# **Electronic Health Record-Based Diagnostic Stewardship to Reduce Antibiotic Use in Asymptomatic Bacteriuria: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocol**

**Protocol Version:** 1.0 (14 November 2026\)  \|  **Target Event:** PIT XXV IPD FK USU 2026  \|  **Source Audit:** [Master Docs (Idea No. 5\)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1W7PuQ0d2bTTk_NcoQBHzBJ4F4DvtDvtIyWicYK2RC60/edit?tab=t.xc79zajfpmjn)  
**Reporting Standard:** PRISMA-P 2015 / SWiM  \|  **Theme Alignment:** Smart Internal Medicine: Bridging Primary and Specialist Care in the Digital Era  
**Protocol Summary & Methodological Guardrail:** This systematic review evaluates whether electronic health record (EHR)-based clinical decision support (CDS) and diagnostic stewardship interventions reduce inappropriate antibiotic treatment among nonpregnant adults with confirmed asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB) without compromising clinical safety. Because published studies use incompatible denominators (confirmed-ASB cases vs. all urine culture orders or patient-days) and heterogeneous quasi-experimental designs, this protocol pre-specifies a design-stratified Synthesis Without Meta-analysis (SWiM) with conditional meta-analysis restricted to methodologically homogeneous strata.

## **1\. Background and Rationale**

Asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB)—defined as isolation of ≥10⁵ CFU/mL of bacteria from urine without urinary tract infection (UTI) symptoms—is routinely overtreated with antibiotics across inpatient, emergency department (ED), and outpatient internal medicine settings. Outside specific guideline-endorsed indications (pregnancy, invasive urological procedures with mucosal bleeding, and early post-renal transplantation), antibiotic treatment of ASB confers no clinical benefit while driving antimicrobial resistance, *Clostridioides difficile* infection, adverse drug events, and excess healthcare costs.  
Electronic health record (EHR) interventions—such as Best Practice Advisories (BPAs), mandatory indication-based order menus, reflex urinalysis-to-culture algorithms, structured symptom calculators, and automated microbiology result nudges—offer scalable diagnostic and antimicrobial stewardship. Prior systematic reviews have not fully answered whether these digital interventions reduce inappropriate antibiotic prescribing specifically among confirmed ASB cases while preserving safety:  
[**Hojat et al. (2023)**](https://doi.org/10.1093/ofid/ofac691) (search cutoff July 2021; 14 studies, 4 pooled) evaluated electronic CDS for UTI diagnosis with urine-culture ordering rate as the primary outcome, leaving downstream confirmed-ASB antibiotic prescribing, patient safety harms, and 2022–2026 studies unaddressed.  
[**Humphrey et al. (2023)**](https://doi.org/10.1177/10600280221134539) (search cutoff May 2021; 13 studies) synthesized broad hospital antimicrobial stewardship interventions for bacteriuria/UTI without isolating electronic/EHR tools or capturing 2021–2026 digital stewardship cohorts.  
[**Yogo et al. (2025)**](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11779270/) (now published as a 10-hospital peer-reviewed full text, PMID 41069222, superseding OFID Abstract P-1583 \[PMC11779270\]) carries a review-like title but is a primary retrospective cohort study evaluating reflex urine culture rules—confirming that no competing 2025–2026 systematic review fills this gap.

## **2\. Review Objectives and PICOS Eligibility Criteria**

The primary objective is to determine the effectiveness and safety of electronic diagnostic stewardship and CDS interventions in reducing inappropriate antibiotic treatment of confirmed ASB among nonpregnant adults. Secondary objectives are to evaluate effects on antibiotic duration (Days of Therapy \[DOT\]), urine culture ordering volume and appropriateness, and clinical safety outcomes. Eligibility criteria follow a strict two-stratum denominator framework summarized below.

| PICOS Domain | Inclusion Criteria | Exclusion Criteria |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Population (P) | Primary stratum: Nonpregnant adults (≥18 years) with confirmed ASB (positive urine culture without UTI-specific symptoms) in inpatient, ED, or outpatient/primary care settings. Secondary diagnostic stratum: Adult encounters with urine-culture orders where testing appropriateness or downstream antibiotic use is measured. | Pediatric populations (\<18 years); pregnant patients; patients undergoing invasive urologic procedures with mucosal bleeding; kidney transplant recipients ≤1 month post-transplant; neutropenic or active non-urinary infection cohorts where antibiotics are clinically indicated. |
| Intervention (I) | EHR-embedded electronic interventions: Best Practice Advisories (BPAs), mandatory indication-based order menus/notes, conditional reflex urinalysis-to-culture rules, EHR-integrated symptom/triage calculators, or automated microbiology comment nudges (alone or bundled with clinician education). | Purely manual/paper educational campaigns, standalone audit-and-feedback, or manual microbiology comments without an EHR/digital workflow component. |
| Comparator (C) | Usual care, concurrent non-intervention control hospitals/units, or a clearly defined pre-intervention baseline period. | Single-arm post-intervention audits without baseline or comparator data. |
| Outcomes (O) | Primary: Proportion of confirmed ASB cases receiving inappropriate antibiotic therapy (any or \>24 h). Secondary: Mean/median DOT or DASC; urine culture ordering rate (per 100 admissions or 1,000 patient-days); culture appropriateness (%); safety balancing metrics (30-day pyelonephritis, urosepsis/GNR bacteremia, ED revisits, hospitalization, mortality, *C. difficile*). | Studies reporting only usability, clinician satisfaction, or theoretical simulation metrics without clinical testing or prescribing outcomes. |
| Study Designs (S) | Randomized controlled trials (including cluster/stepped-wedge), controlled before–after (CBA) studies, difference-in-differences (DiD) cohorts, interrupted time series (ITS), and clearly labeled quasi-experimental pre–post cohorts. | Case reports, narrative reviews, editorials, qualitative-only studies, and protocols without completed outcome data. |

Note on conference abstracts: Peer-reviewed full-text articles will form the primary evidence base. Conference abstracts (e.g., Yogo et al., 2025\) will be tracked separately in sensitivity/grey-literature tables unless a corresponding full-text article is identified.

## **3\. Information Sources and Search Strategy**

A comprehensive literature search will be executed across PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, CINAHL, and Web of Science from database inception through November 2026 without language restrictions (non-English articles translated as needed). ClinicalTrials.gov and PROSPERO will be searched for ongoing or recently completed trials, and backward/forward citation chaining will be performed on all included studies and key prior reviews (Hojat 2023; Humphrey 2023).

## **4\. Study Selection, Deduplication, and Data Extraction**

Two reviewers will independently screen titles/abstracts and full-text articles against the PICOS criteria, resolving disagreements by consensus or a third senior reviewer. A standardized extraction form will capture study setting, design, sample size, ASB case definition, exclusion criteria, electronic intervention mechanics (ordering-stage vs. laboratory-reflex vs. post-result nudge), educational co-interventions, denominator type, primary/secondary outcomes, and safety follow-up.  
**Cohort-Lineage Deduplication Rule:** Studies sharing the same hospital network and overlapping intervention periods must not be double-counted. Six verified institutional lineages and duplicate-publication families are linked prospectively so overlapping cohorts are never double-counted: (1) Riyadh Medical Center (Alghamdi et al., 2025 \[PMID 40700033\] → Alghamdi et al., 2026 \[PMID 42211652\]); (2) University of Nebraska Medical Center (UNMC) (Osiemo et al., 2021 \[PMID 34449690\] → Torres et al., 2025 \[PMID 39943701\] → Keintz et al., 2026 \[PMID 42870557\]; with pre-intervention single-arm audit Keintz et al., 2026 \[PMID 41772991\] excluded); (3) VA Practice-Based Research Network (Claeys et al., 2021 \[PMID 32838829\] → Claeys et al., 2026 \[PMID 40720743\]); (4) Emory Healthcare (Howard-Anderson et al., 2020 \[PMID 31996274\] → conference abstract Kim et al., 2024 \[PMC11504427\]); (5) Washington University – Barnes-Jewish Hospital (Munigala et al., 2018 \[PMID 29353243\] → Munigala et al., 2019 \[PMID 30786940\]); and (6) Ohio Health System (peer-reviewed full text Yogo et al., 2025 \[PMID 41069222\] superseding conference abstract PMC11779270).

## **5\. Risk of Bias Assessment**

Risk of bias will be appraised independently by two reviewers using design-appropriate validated instruments:

* Randomized / Cluster-Randomized Trials: Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2\) tool.  
* Non-Randomized Controlled / Pre–Post Cohorts: ROBINS-I (Risk Of Bias In Non-randomised Studies of Interventions), with explicit attention to confounding, secular trends, and co-interventions (education vs. EHR rule).  
* Interrupted Time Series (ITS): Cochrane EPOC ITS quality criteria (independence of intervention from concurrent changes, pre-specified shape of effect, autocorrelation testing, and ≥3 data points pre/post).

## **6\. Data Synthesis and Analysis Plan (Design-Stratified SWiM)**

A single pooled meta-analysis across all included studies is methodologically inappropriate because studies report incompatible denominators (confirmed ASB patients vs. all urine-culture orders vs. 1,000 patient-days) and use non-comparable designs (ITS slopes vs. unadjusted pre–post proportions). Therefore, synthesis will follow the Synthesis Without Meta-analysis (SWiM) guideline, stratified into three pre-specified analytic groups:

1. Stratum A — Patient-Level Confirmed-ASB Antibiotic Prescribing: Studies reporting inappropriate antibiotic use (\>24 h or any) among independently verified ASB patients. Relative risks (RR) with 95% confidence intervals will be computed; random-effects meta-analysis will only be performed if ≥3 studies share comparable designs, ASB definitions, and follow-up windows (heterogeneity assessed via I² and τ²).  
2. Stratum B — Interrupted Time Series (ITS) Diagnostic & Prescribing Trends: Studies reporting segmented regression level changes and monthly slope changes (e.g., urine cultures per 100 admissions or 1,000 patient-days). Results will be tabulated and visualized via effect-direction/forest-style summary plots without pooling raw slopes across differing denominators.  
3. Stratum C — Safety & Balancing Measures: 30-day pyelonephritis, Gram-negative bacteremia/urosepsis, ED revisits, mortality, and *C. difficile* rates will be synthesized narratively alongside prescribing outcomes to ensure diagnostic stewardship does not cause under-treatment harm.
